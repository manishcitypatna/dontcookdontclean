## Why Multi-Generational Households Need a Different Hiring Approach

When a household spans grandparents, parents, and children, a single helper's role often ends up covering a wider range of tasks than in a smaller nuclear-family home — cooking for different dietary needs, general housekeeping, and elder care all at once. That combination requires a broader skill set than a typical part-time cleaning role, and it's worth being explicit about all of it during hiring rather than discovering gaps later.

Older buildings in Kadamkuan also mean layouts and household routines that have often been set for years — new help needs to adapt to an established rhythm, not the other way around.

## What Families in Kadamkuan Commonly Get Wrong When Hiring

### Under-Describing the Full Scope of a Multi-Generational Role
If the role covers cooking for different dietary needs plus elder care plus general housekeeping, say so clearly at the outset. A vague job description leads to a mismatch discovered only after the helper starts.

### Assuming Elder-Care Experience Is a Given
Not every candidate with general housekeeping experience has handled elder care specifically. Ask directly and, where possible, get a reference focused on that part of the role.

### Not Involving the Elder Family Member in the Hiring Decision
Where the helper will be spending significant time with an elderly household member, it helps to involve that person in at least part of the hiring conversation — fit matters as much as skill here.
